Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

Psychrometry is the science of how air temperature, humidity and moisture interact, and the IICRC-certified technicians who dry structures for Morristown Restoration know it well. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Let air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, 24/7, until the technician removes them
  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted
  • Address any water intrusion immediately, because the faster drying begins, the better the outcome
  • If a new damp spot or smell shows up while drying is underway, let the technician know at once
  • Go by the meter, not by touch, since a dry-feeling surface may still hold moisture deep inside
  • Air movers need a clear path, so avoid stacking things on the equipment or in front of it

What a Professional Brings to Structural Drying

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

Signs You Need Structural Drying in Morristown

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Musty odors that continue after extraction, which suggests trapped moisture
  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet
  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards
  • Warped casing or baseboard where water reached the bottom of the wall
  • Ceilings, floors or walls that still read high on a moisture meter following a leak
  • Earlier water damage that was never professionally dried and now shows deterioration
