Bathroom Water Damage in Morristown

Bathrooms are built to get wet on the surface, not underneath it. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. Hidden from view, it builds up behind the wall tile and down in the subfloor. In Morristown, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet area without tearing out more than we need to, then open only the sections that cannot be dried in place.

What It Looks Like

  • Grout lines that stay dark and damp long after the last shower
  • Tiles near the tub that have loosened, shift when stepped on, or sound hollow
  • Directly under the bathroom, a ceiling with a brown stain or a sag
  • Floor that feels spongy by the vanity or next to the tub
  • Caulk along the tub or shower that is cracked, peeling, or black with mold
  • Inside the vanity, a base shelf that is stained, soft or out of shape
What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Poor Ventilation

Without a working exhaust fan, the steam from showers has nowhere to go. It condenses on the ceiling and walls, encourages mold, and keeps already wet materials from drying.

A Failed Shower Pan

Every use of the shower sends water beneath its floor when the pan or liner is cracked. Framing under the shower stays saturated even though the surface shows no flaw.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Supply valves and drain fittings under the sink can drip for months behind stored bottles and towels. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.

Mold in the Tiled Wall

Neither grout nor tile is a waterproof barrier without help. Mold takes hold on the wall board once that hidden layer remains wet, and no scrubbing can get to it.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

First days

Water Gets Past

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. Backer board and the outer rim of the subfloor take it in, and there is still nothing to see.

The first several weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

Months one to three

Signs Show Up

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months and beyond

The Structure Suffers

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Pinpoint the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Open What Is Needed

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. If the toilet was the source, we handle that section as contaminated, and porous materials we cannot clean go.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

The subfloor, the framing in the walls and the space above the lower ceiling are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

We replace subfloor and backer board, and can coordinate tile, a new toilet seal, fresh caulk and ceiling repair so the leak path is closed, not covered over.
